## 2. Volume Contents & Location of Practice Texts

### MW2CZ7988 (15-vol Derge Edition)
The outline O2KG3 exists but detailed per-volume TOC could not be retrieved via API. The 15 volumes contain Shardza's "Five Treasures" (mdzod lnga):

Based on scholarly literature (Achard, HimalayanArt.org), the Five Treasures are:
1. **Legs bshad rin po che'i mdzod** — Treasury of Precious Teachings (general Buddhist/Bon philosophy)
2. **Lung rigs rin po che'i mdzod** — Treasury of Scripture and Reasoning
3. **sNyan rgyud rin po che'i mdzod** — Treasury of the Oral Transmission (Dzogchen)
4. **Phyogs med rin po che'i mdzod** — Impartial Treasury
5. **gSung thor bu** — Miscellaneous writings

## 4. OCR Feasibility Assessment

### Scan Quality by Edition

| Edition | BDRC ID | Format | OCR Prospects |
|---------|---------|--------|---------------|
| Derge woodblock (15 vol) | W2CZ7988 | Woodblock prints → TIFF scans | **MODERATE** — Original woodblock prints tend to have clear carving but can have ink inconsistencies. TIFF format suggests higher resolution. Thumbnail available. |
| Expanded edition (18 vol) | W4CZ58530 | Woodblock prints → JPG scans | **MODERATE to GOOD** — 18 volumes (10,422 pp.) with JPG thumbnails. May be a later, cleaner print run. |
| Dehra Dun reprint (1 vol) | W23421 | Woodblock reprint → TIFF | **MODERATE** — Indian exile reprint, quality can vary. |

### General OCR Challenges for Bon Woodblock Texts
1. **Woodblock printing** produces variable ink density across pages
2. **Dbu med script** (cursive) vs **dbu can** (headed) — woodblock gsung 'bum typically use dbu can, which is more OCR-friendly
3. **Page margins, annotations, and interlinear notes** may confuse OCR
4. **Previous Vol 3 OCR failure** likely due to: faded ink, damage to blocks, poor scan exposure, or heavy marginal annotations
5. **Recommendation**: Try W4CZ58530 (18-vol edition) first — likely a cleaner print run with more pages suggesting supplementary materials

### Tools for Tibetan OCR
- **Google's Tibetan OCR** via Cloud Vision API (limited accuracy)
- **Namsel OCR** — BDRC's own Tibetan OCR tool (best option, open source)
- **OpenPecha** project — BDRC-affiliated, working on Tibetan text digitization
- Manual transliteration remains most reliable for critical editions

## 5. A-khrid Text Sources and Availability

### What is A-khrid?
The A-khrid (ཨ་ཁྲིད) is a Bon meditation system organized into **80 sessions** (*thun*, also described as 80 "steps"), systematized in the 11th century. It is one of the three main Dzogchen transmission lineages in Bon (alongside Zhang Zhung Nyan Gyud and Dzogchen).

### A-khrid on BDRC
- A-khrid texts are distributed across multiple collections
- Shardza's writings include A-khrid commentaries within his gsung 'bum
- Standalone A-khrid texts exist from earlier lineage holders

### Published Translations/Studies of A-khrid
| Work | Author | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| *Heart Drops of Dharmakaya* | Lopon Tenzin Namdak / Shardza | Includes A-khrid-related Dzogchen practice |
| Per Kværne's studies on Bon | Per Kværne | Pioneer Western scholar of Bon; discusses A-khrid lineage |
| Jean-Luc Achard's work on A-khrid | Achard | Multiple articles in Revue d'Etudes Tibétaines |

### Digital Availability
- **No known fully digitized/transliterated** A-khrid texts in Unicode Tibetan on BDRC
- BDRC holdings are scanned images (PDF/TIFF/JPG of woodblock prints and manuscripts)
- **OpenPecha** may have some texts in progress — check `github.com/OpenPecha`
- **84000.co** focuses on Buddhist canonical translations, not Bon texts
- **Shenten Dargye Ling** (Lopon Tenzin Namdak's center in France) may have internal transliterations
